1.2 Delegation and impersonation

These are different and the distinction is load bearing.

In *impersonation*, the resulting token is indistinguishable from one issued to
the subject directly. The resource cannot tell that an intermediary was
involved, and the audit trail records only the subject.

In *delegation*, the token names both parties: the subject on whose behalf the
request is made, and the actor making it. A resource can then apply policy to
the pair, and an audit trail records which agent acted for which person.

Deployments SHOULD prefer delegation. Impersonation discards the only piece of
information that makes an automated actor accountable, and it does so at exactly
the point where accountability is most useful.
